# MODERATION TEAM HANDBOOK
### Author: Its-Aurelia-Dev

**Server Staff Document — Moderation Team**

## 1. PURPOSE

The Moderator Team is responsible for keeping the server safe, organized, and enjoyable for everyone.

Moderators are expected to enforce the public `SERVER RULES` consistently, professionally, and without personal bias.

Being a moderator is a responsibility, not a status symbol. Moderator permissions must never be used to win arguments, punish people out of spite, or give friends special treatment.

---

# 2. MODERATOR RESPONSIBILITIES

Moderators are responsible for:

* Monitoring public channels.
* Responding to member reports.
* Enforcing server rules.
* Handling spam and disruptive behavior.
* Issuing warnings and punishments.
* Collecting evidence when necessary.
* Helping members understand server rules.
* Reporting serious incidents to Admins.
* Reporting bugs and exploits to the appropriate staff/development team.
* Keeping moderation actions professional and confidential.
* Helping maintain a welcoming community.

Moderators are **not** responsible for making major policy decisions, changing server permissions, managing other staff members, or making significant technical changes unless an Admin has specifically authorized them.

---

# 3. CORE STAFF PRINCIPLES

Every moderation action should follow these principles:

### Be Fair

Do not punish someone because you dislike them.

Do not give friends lighter punishments simply because you know them.

Do not give someone harsher punishment because of previous arguments.

### Be Consistent

Similar situations should generally receive similar punishments.

Consider context when deciding whether a situation is minor, repeated, or severe.

### Be Professional

Do not argue with members.

Do not insult people.

Do not threaten members with punishments.

Do not use moderation actions to make a point.

### Be Calm

If a member becomes angry, remain calm.

You are expected to de-escalate situations rather than make them worse.

### Be Accountable

If you make a moderation mistake, tell an Admin.

Do not delete evidence or hide mistakes simply to avoid consequences.

---

# 4. PUBLIC SERVER RULES

Moderators enforce the server's public rules, including:

1. Respect members and staff.
2. No harmful content.
3. No piracy.
4. Keep chat appropriate.
5. Respect staff.
6. Report problems properly.
7. No bot abuse.
8. Keep profiles appropriate.
9. No cheating or exploiting.
10. Report bugs properly.
11. No ban evasion.
12. Use common sense.

The full public rules should always take priority over personal interpretations of what is acceptable.

---

# 5. STANDARD PUNISHMENT SYSTEM

The standard moderation progression is:

**1st violation:** 30-minute mute + warning

**2nd violation:** 1-hour mute + warning

**3rd violation:** 24-hour mute + final warning

Further violations may result in longer punishments or removal from the server.

Severe violations may result in an immediate ban.

Staff may adjust punishments depending on the seriousness, context, history, and circumstances of the incident.

---

# 6. WARNINGS

A warning should clearly communicate:

* What rule was broken.
* What behavior needs to stop.
* What punishment was issued.
* What may happen if the behavior continues.

A warning should not be insulting or argumentative.

### Example

> You have been warned for repeated spam. Please stop flooding the channel. Continued violations may result in additional moderation action.

Do not publicly argue about whether a warning was deserved.

---

# 7. MUTES

Use mutes when a member is disrupting chat or violating a rule that can be addressed by temporarily restricting their ability to communicate.

Before muting:

1. Confirm the behavior.
2. Check whether there is previous relevant moderation history.
3. Save evidence when appropriate.
4. Apply the appropriate punishment.
5. Record the action if the server uses moderation logs.
6. Move on unless further action is required.

Do not repeatedly extend a mute because a member complains about it.

---

# 8. KICKS AND BANS

Moderators should normally escalate permanent or serious removals to an Admin unless the server has specifically granted Mods ban permissions.

Immediate escalation is appropriate for:

* Doxxing or attempts to expose private information.
* Serious threats.
* Hate speech or severe discrimination.
* Malicious files or links.
* Serious impersonation.
* Ban evasion.
* Severe harassment.
* Serious cheating or exploit abuse.
* Repeated serious rule violations.
* Behavior that creates an immediate safety risk.

When escalating, provide the Admin with the facts and evidence rather than personal opinions.

---

# 9. REPORT HANDLING

When someone submits a report:

### Step 1 — Read the report

Understand what the member is reporting before taking action.

### Step 2 — Gather evidence

Look at relevant messages, screenshots, logs, or other available evidence.

Do not rely solely on rumors.

### Step 3 — Determine the rule

Identify which server rule applies.

### Step 4 — Check history

Look for relevant previous punishments when the situation involves repeated behavior.

### Step 5 — Take action

Apply the appropriate punishment or escalate to an Admin.

### Step 6 — Document

Record the action when required by the server's staff system.

### Step 7 — Close the report

Do not unnecessarily discuss private moderation information with the reporter.

---

# 10. PUBLIC ARGUMENTS

If two members begin arguing:

1. Ask them to stop.
2. Move the discussion away from public channels if appropriate.
3. Warn or mute members if they continue breaking rules.
4. Avoid taking sides in personal disputes.
5. Escalate serious harassment or threats.

Moderators should not become participants in member arguments.

---

# 11. STAFF HARASSMENT

Members are allowed to disagree with moderation decisions.

They are not allowed to harass, threaten, spam, or repeatedly DM/tag staff.

If a member has a legitimate concern:

* Direct them to the proper support/report channel.
* Explain the appeal process if one exists.
* Do not argue through DMs.

---

# 12. BAN EVASION

If a banned member returns using an alternate account or another method:

1. Gather evidence.
2. Do not publicly accuse the person without reasonable evidence.
3. Report the suspected evasion to an Admin.
4. Allow an Admin to make the final determination if required.

Do not encourage members to investigate someone's private information.

---

# 13. CHEATING AND EXPLOITS

If someone is suspected of cheating or abusing an exploit:

* Do not encourage them to demonstrate the exploit publicly.
* Do not distribute exploit instructions.
* Save relevant evidence.
* Report the issue to the appropriate Admin or Developer.
* Take moderation action when the evidence supports it.

If the issue involves a technical vulnerability, prioritize reporting it privately.

---

# 14. BUG REPORTS

Moderators should direct members to the official bug-report system.

Useful information includes:

* What happened.
* Where it happened.
* What the member was doing.
* Approximate time.
* Screenshots or video when appropriate.
* Steps that reproduce the issue, provided those steps do not expose a dangerous exploit.

Do not promise that a bug will be fixed.

---

# 15. BOT ABUSE

Watch for:

* Command spam.
* Repeated failed commands.
* Attempts to overwhelm bots.
* Abuse of automated features.
* Attempts to bypass bot restrictions.

If a bot appears to malfunction, notify an Admin or Developer rather than repeatedly testing it in production.

---

# 16. MODERATION EVIDENCE

Evidence should be:

* Relevant.
* Accurate.
* Unedited when possible.
* Stored in the appropriate staff channel or logging system.
* Kept confidential.

Do not share private reports or moderation evidence with unrelated members.

---

# 17. CONFLICT OF INTEREST

If you are personally involved in a situation, step back whenever possible.

Examples:

* The member is your close friend.
* You are currently arguing with the member.
* The report concerns another staff member you have a personal conflict with.
* You were directly involved in the incident.

Ask another Moderator or Admin to handle the situation.

---

# 18. MODERATOR PERMISSION ABUSE

The following are prohibited:

* Punishing members for personal reasons.
* Punishing members for criticizing staff respectfully.
* Giving friends special treatment.
* Threatening members with staff permissions.
* Using private information against members.
* Sharing confidential staff information.
* Using bots or permissions unnecessarily.
* Deleting evidence to hide misconduct.
* Changing moderation records dishonestly.

Permission abuse may result in removal from the staff team.

---

# 19. STAFF COMMUNICATION

Use staff channels for moderation discussions.

Keep staff discussions:

* Relevant.
* Respectful.
* Evidence-based.
* Confidential.

Do not turn staff channels into public drama.

When escalating a situation, include:

**Member:**
**Rule:**
**What happened:**
**Evidence:**
**Previous punishment:**
**Action taken:**
**What you need from Admins:**

---

# 20. MODERATOR COMMANDS

Use only commands officially provided by the server.

Do not experiment with unknown moderation commands on real members.

Typical command categories may include:

* Warning.
* Mute/timeout.
* Kick.
* Ban.
* Unban.
* User information.
* Moderation history.
* Report management.
* Logging.

If you are unsure what a command does, ask an Admin before using it.

---

# 21. WHEN TO CONTACT AN ADMIN

Contact an Admin when:

* A permanent ban may be necessary.
* A member is appealing a serious punishment.
* Another staff member may have abused permissions.
* You are unsure how to handle a serious incident.
* There is a serious threat or safety concern.
* There is suspected ban evasion.
* There is a serious exploit.
* There is a conflict involving staff.
* You believe the normal punishment system is not appropriate for the situation.

When in doubt, escalate rather than guess.

---

# 22. MODERATOR CHECKLIST

Before taking action, ask:

* Did the member actually break a rule?
* Do I have enough evidence?
* Am I being fair?
* Am I following the punishment system?
* Is there relevant previous history?
* Is this something an Admin should handle?
* Am I acting professionally?

If the answer to these questions is satisfactory, take the appropriate action and document it.

---

# 23. FINAL STANDARD

A good Moderator does not try to punish the most people.

A good Moderator keeps the community safe, handles problems calmly, applies rules fairly, and knows when to ask for help.

Moderator permissions are a responsibility and can be removed if they are abused.
