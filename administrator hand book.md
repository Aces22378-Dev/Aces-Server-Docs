# ADMINISTRATOR HANDBOOK
### Author: Its-Aurelia-Dev

**Server Staff Document — Administrator Team**

## 1. PURPOSE

Administrators are responsible for overseeing the server's moderation system, staff team, community safety, and major administrative decisions.

Admins have greater permissions than Moderators and therefore have a greater responsibility to use those permissions carefully.

An Admin should be a problem solver, not simply a Moderator with more commands.

---

# 2. ADMIN RESPONSIBILITIES

Admins are responsible for:

* Overseeing Moderators.
* Handling serious incidents.
* Reviewing appeals.
* Managing major punishments.
* Investigating staff misconduct.
* Managing staff permissions.
* Training and supervising staff.
* Handling ban evasion.
* Coordinating with Developers on technical issues.
* Protecting confidential information.
* Reviewing moderation consistency.
* Escalating major issues to the Server Owner when necessary.

---

# 3. ADMIN AUTHORITY

Admins may generally:

* Review moderation records.
* Review reports.
* Adjust punishments when justified.
* Approve serious bans.
* Handle staff complaints.
* Remove or restrict staff permissions.
* Escalate technical problems.
* Coordinate emergency responses.

Admins should not make major permanent changes to the server's structure, ownership, or core systems without appropriate authorization from the Server Owner.

---

# 4. ADMIN PRINCIPLES

### Fairness

Admins must remain neutral when possible.

A member's popularity, rank, friendships, or reputation should not determine whether rules apply.

### Consistency

Review whether punishments are being applied consistently across the community.

### Accountability

Admins are still subject to staff rules.

Having administrator permissions does not make someone exempt from consequences.

### Confidentiality

Reports, appeals, staff discussions, and private information should remain within the appropriate staff areas.

### Minimal Access

Use only the permissions necessary to accomplish the task.

---

# 5. MODERATION SYSTEM

The standard punishment progression is:

**1st violation:** 30-minute mute + warning

**2nd violation:** 1-hour mute + warning

**3rd violation:** 24-hour mute + final warning

Further violations may result in longer punishments or removal.

Severe violations may result in an immediate ban.

Admins may adjust this system when circumstances justify doing so.

---

# 6. PUNISHMENT OVERRIDES

An Admin may increase or decrease a punishment based on:

* Severity.
* Repeated behavior.
* Intent.
* Whether the member immediately stopped.
* Whether there was a credible threat.
* Whether other members were targeted.
* Whether the behavior created significant disruption.
* Whether the standard punishment would be unreasonable for the situation.

Any major deviation from the standard system should be documented.

---

# 7. SERIOUS VIOLATIONS

Admins should immediately review situations involving:

* Doxxing.
* Serious threats.
* Hate speech or severe discrimination.
* Malicious files or links.
* Serious harassment.
* Ban evasion.
* Severe impersonation.
* Serious cheating.
* Intentional exploit abuse.
* Repeated serious violations.
* Staff permission abuse.

When necessary, restrict the account while the situation is investigated.

---

# 8. BAN PROCEDURE

Before issuing a permanent ban when circumstances allow:

1. Confirm the rule violation.
2. Review available evidence.
3. Check relevant moderation history.
4. Determine whether the situation requires immediate removal.
5. Record the reason.
6. Apply the ban.
7. Document the decision.
8. Provide appeal information when appropriate.

For urgent severe violations, immediate action may be taken first and reviewed afterward.

---

# 9. BAN APPEALS

Appeals should be reviewed objectively.

An appeal should generally include:

* The user's account information.
* The punishment received.
* The reason for the appeal.
* Any relevant explanation or evidence.

Possible outcomes:

* Punishment upheld.
* Punishment reduced.
* Punishment removed.
* Appeal denied with permission to appeal again later.

Do not promise a specific outcome.

A member should not receive special treatment because they know an Admin personally.

---

# 10. STAFF MANAGEMENT

Admins are responsible for maintaining a functional staff team.

Admins should:

* Train new Moderators.
* Explain expectations.
* Monitor staff performance.
* Address repeated mistakes.
* Recognize responsible staff behavior.
* Investigate complaints.
* Remove permissions when necessary.

Staff promotions should be based on reliability, judgment, activity, and conduct rather than popularity alone.

---

# 11. STAFF MISCONDUCT

Examples include:

* Abuse of permissions.
* Favoritism.
* Retaliatory punishments.
* Sharing confidential information.
* Harassing members.
* Misusing bots.
* Manipulating moderation logs.
* Using staff privileges for personal benefit.
* Ignoring serious violations because of friendships.
* Intentionally interfering with another staff member's work.

Depending on severity, consequences may include:

* Coaching.
* Formal warning.
* Temporary permission restriction.
* Demotion.
* Removal from staff.
* Permanent staff blacklist.

Serious cases should be documented.

---

# 12. STAFF DISPUTES

If staff members disagree:

1. Move the discussion to the appropriate staff area.
2. Allow each person to explain their position.
3. Review evidence.
4. Focus on the server rather than personal disagreements.
5. Make a decision when necessary.
6. Record major decisions.

Do not allow staff disagreements to become public drama.

---

# 13. REPORT ESCALATION

When a Moderator escalates a report, the Admin should determine:

* What happened?
* Which rule applies?
* What evidence exists?
* Has the member been punished previously?
* Was the Moderator's response appropriate?
* Is additional action necessary?

Do not automatically reverse a Moderator's action simply because a member complains.

---

# 14. BAN EVASION

When ban evasion is suspected:

1. Review available evidence.
2. Compare relevant account information and behavior.
3. Avoid publicly exposing private information.
4. Confirm the connection before taking major action when possible.
5. Document the determination.
6. Take appropriate action.

Do not encourage staff or members to investigate private information outside legitimate server tools.

---

# 15. EXPLOITS AND CHEATING

Admins should coordinate with Developers when a technical exploit is reported.

Do not encourage members to spread exploit instructions.

If an exploit can seriously affect the server:

* Limit access to the affected feature when possible.
* Preserve relevant evidence.
* Notify Developers.
* Determine whether affected users require moderation action.
* Communicate publicly only when necessary.

---

# 16. TECHNICAL INCIDENTS

Examples include:

* Bots malfunctioning.
* Permissions being incorrectly assigned.
* Channels disappearing.
* Unauthorized access.
* Data loss.
* Exploit reports.
* Server configuration problems.

Admins should avoid making unnecessary technical changes.

If the issue is development-related, contact the Developer team.

---

# 17. PERMISSIONS AND SECURITY

Admin permissions should be treated as sensitive.

Admins must:

* Never share credentials.
* Never give permissions to unapproved users.
* Avoid unnecessary administrator-level access.
* Review suspicious permission changes.
* Report compromised accounts immediately.
* Follow the server's authentication and security procedures.

If an Admin account may be compromised, notify the Server Owner immediately and secure the account.

---

# 18. CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION

Confidential information includes:

* Staff discussions.
* Private reports.
* Appeal information.
* Internal moderation records.
* Private user information.
* Security information.
* Developer credentials or secrets.

Do not share confidential information with regular members.

---

# 19. MODERATION REVIEW

Admins should periodically review:

* Punishment consistency.
* Staff activity.
* Repeat offenders.
* Unresolved reports.
* Appeals.
* Staff complaints.
* Permission assignments.

The purpose is not to micromanage staff. It is to identify problems before they become larger problems.

---

# 20. WORKING WITH DEVELOPERS

Admins should provide Developers with clear technical reports.

Include:

**Issue:**
**Affected feature:**
**What happened:**
**When it happened:**
**Evidence:**
**How many users appear affected:**
**Severity:**
**Immediate action taken:**

Admins should not demand unnecessary technical details from Developers or make production changes themselves unless authorized.

---

# 21. EMERGENCY RESPONSE

For a serious incident:

1. Stay calm.
2. Protect the community.
3. Stop ongoing harmful behavior when possible.
4. Preserve evidence.
5. Notify other appropriate staff.
6. Restrict affected accounts or features when necessary.
7. Coordinate with Developers if technical systems are involved.
8. Document what happened.
9. Escalate to the Owner when required.

Avoid speculation and public accusations.

---

# 22. ADMIN DECISION STANDARD

Before making a major decision, ask:

* Is this necessary?
* Is there evidence?
* Is it consistent with the rules?
* Is the punishment proportional?
* Does another staff member need to be involved?
* Does the Server Owner need to know?
* Have I documented the decision?

---

# 23. FINAL STANDARD

Admins are responsible for setting the standard for the rest of the staff team.

A good Admin is fair, calm, accountable, and willing to admit when they are wrong.

Greater permissions mean greater responsibility—not immunity from the rules.
