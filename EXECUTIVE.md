# EXECUTIVE HANDBOOK

**Author:** Its-Aurelia-Dev
**Server Staff Document — Executive Team**

## 1. PURPOSE

The Executive Team is responsible for the overall direction, management, and long-term success of the server.

Executives oversee major operations and coordinate between departments.

Executive authority should be used to protect the server's long-term interests rather than for personal influence.

---

# 2. EXECUTIVE RESPONSIBILITIES

Executives may be responsible for:

* Server-wide strategy.
* Department oversight.
* Major policy decisions.
* Staff leadership.
* Organizational planning.
* Major partnerships.
* Project approval.
* Resource management.
* Handling major incidents.
* Reviewing department performance.
* Coordinating Admins, Developers, QA, and Community Staff.
* Reporting major matters to the Server Owner.

---

# 3. EXECUTIVE STRUCTURE

Executives should understand the responsibilities of each department.

### Moderators

Handle day-to-day rule enforcement.

### Administrators

Handle serious moderation, staff management, appeals, and administrative operations.

### Developers

Build and maintain technical systems.

### QA

Test systems and verify bugs and fixes.

### Community Staff

Focus on member experience, engagement, and community development.

### Executives

Coordinate the organization and make major strategic decisions.

---

# 4. LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES

### Accountability

Executives are responsible for their decisions.

### Transparency

Important decisions should have a clear reason.

### Fairness

Do not allow personal relationships to determine staff decisions.

### Stability

Avoid unnecessary changes that create confusion or disruption.

### Long-Term Thinking

Consider how decisions will affect the server weeks or months from now, not only today.

---

# 5. EXECUTIVE AUTHORITY

Executives may make major organizational decisions within the authority granted to them by the Server Owner.

This may include:

* Approving projects.
* Restructuring departments.
* Approving staff promotions or removals.
* Establishing internal procedures.
* Coordinating major events.
* Approving significant server changes.
* Managing organizational priorities.

Executives should not assume authority over the Owner unless explicitly authorized.

---

# 6. DEPARTMENT OVERSIGHT

Executives should monitor whether departments are functioning properly.

This includes reviewing:

* Staff performance.
* Development progress.
* QA results.
* Community feedback.
* Moderation consistency.
* Outstanding issues.
* Major projects.

Executives should avoid micromanaging individual staff members.

---

# 7. STAFF PROMOTIONS

Promotions should consider:

* Reliability.
* Activity.
* Judgment.
* Conduct.
* Communication.
* Knowledge of the role.
* Ability to handle responsibility.

Promotions should not be based solely on friendship or popularity.

---

# 8. STAFF REMOVALS

Staff may be removed or demoted for:

* Permission abuse.
* Serious misconduct.
* Repeated failure to perform responsibilities.
* Confidentiality violations.
* Inactivity when the role requires activity.
* Dishonesty.
* Serious violations of server policy.

Major removals should be documented.

---

# 9. MAJOR PROJECTS

Before approving a major project, consider:

**Purpose:**
What problem does it solve?

**Benefit:**
How does it improve the server?

**Cost:**
What resources are required?

**Risk:**
What could go wrong?

**Staffing:**
Who will build, test, and manage it?

**Timeline:**
When should it be completed?

**Maintenance:**
Who will maintain it afterward?

---

# 10. WORKING WITH DEVELOPERS

Executives should establish the desired outcome rather than dictate unnecessary technical implementation details.

Developers should determine the appropriate technical solution.

Executives should ask:

* What is being built?
* Why is it needed?
* What resources are required?
* What risks exist?
* How will it be tested?
* What is the maintenance plan?

---

# 11. WORKING WITH QA

QA should be involved before major features are released.

Executives should not pressure QA to mark an unfinished or broken system as passed.

If QA reports a serious issue, the issue should be addressed before release when practical.

---

# 12. WORKING WITH COMMUNITY STAFF

Community Staff provide insight into member experience.

Executives should pay attention to recurring community feedback.

However, feedback should be evaluated rather than automatically treated as a demand.

---

# 13. WORKING WITH ADMINS

Admins handle the operational side of moderation.

Executives should allow Admins to manage normal moderation without unnecessary interference.

Executive involvement is appropriate for:

* Major staff disputes.
* Serious organizational issues.
* Major policy changes.
* Significant moderation controversies.
* Repeated staff problems.
* Situations affecting the server's overall direction.

---

# 14. MAJOR INCIDENTS

For a major server incident:

1. Establish what happened.
2. Identify the responsible department.
3. Assign appropriate staff.
4. Protect members and server systems.
5. Coordinate with Admins and Developers.
6. Establish clear communication.
7. Monitor the response.
8. Document the incident.
9. Review what can be improved afterward.

Do not allow multiple departments to make conflicting changes without coordination.

---

# 15. PUBLIC COMMUNICATION

Executives may be responsible for major announcements.

Public statements should be:

* Accurate.
* Clear.
* Professional.
* Necessary.
* Consistent with internal information.

Do not publicly blame individual staff members for internal problems.

---

# 16. CONFIDENTIALITY

Executives may have access to highly sensitive information.

This may include:

* Staff records.
* Internal discussions.
* Security information.
* Development plans.
* Private member reports.
* Organizational plans.
* Partnership information.

Such information must be protected.

---

# 17. CONFLICT OF INTEREST

Executives should disclose conflicts of interest when appropriate.

Examples include:

* Personal relationships affecting staff decisions.
* Financial interests.
* Personal disputes.
* Projects that directly benefit the Executive.

Another Executive or the Server Owner may need to handle the decision instead.

---

# 18. EMERGENCY AUTHORITY

During an emergency, an Executive may need to make rapid decisions.

Emergency decisions should prioritize:

1. Member safety.
2. Server security.
3. Service stability.
4. Preservation of important data.
5. Clear communication.

After the emergency, decisions should be reviewed and documented.

---

# 19. EXECUTIVE ACCOUNTABILITY

Executives are not above the server's rules or internal standards.

Executive misconduct may include:

* Abuse of authority.
* Retaliation.
* Favoritism.
* Unauthorized access.
* Financial or resource misuse.
* Confidentiality violations.
* Sabotage.
* Manipulation of staff.
* Intentionally misleading members or staff.

Serious executive misconduct should be escalated to the Server Owner or the authority responsible for executive oversight.

---

# 20. DECISION-MAKING STANDARD

Before making a major decision, consider:

* Does this benefit the server?
* Is there enough information?
* Who will be affected?
* Have the relevant departments been consulted?
* What are the risks?
* Is there a better alternative?
* Does the Server Owner need to approve it?
* Can the decision be reversed if necessary?
* Has the decision been documented?

---

# 21. EXECUTIVE MEETINGS

Major meetings should have:

* A clear purpose.
* Relevant participants.
* Defined topics.
* Decisions recorded afterward.
* Assigned responsibilities.

A useful meeting record includes:

**Topic:**
**Decision:**
**Reason:**
**Responsible department:**
**Deadline:**
**Follow-up:**

---

# 22. FINAL STANDARD

Executives are responsible for the direction and stability of the organization.

A good Executive does not simply have authority. They use that authority responsibly, coordinate departments effectively, protect the community, support staff, and make decisions with the long-term health of the server in mind.
